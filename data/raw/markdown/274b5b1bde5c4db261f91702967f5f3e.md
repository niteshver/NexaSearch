# Reliability Scales Inversely: Hallucinations Snowball Faster in Bigger Language Models
Kushal Chakrabarti Obviously Wrong, LLC San Francisco, CA 94107 kushalc@obviouslywrong.org
###### Abstract
Bigger language models are less reliable. Across three families, three benchmarks and six rungs, including in-the-wild chat logs, scaling closes the start-of-response knowledge gap up to while within-response knowledge _degradation_ grows up to . We trace that residual to one variable, the per-position disagreement δ=\delta=\log p_{M}-\log p_{O} against a stronger oracle, whose second moment splits exactly into _bias_ 2 \mathrm{KL}(p_{M}\,\|\,p_{O})^{2} and _decoding risk_ \mathrm{Var}[\delta]. That split is an interpretability statement before it is a statistical one: the model’s self-readable uncertainty H(p_{M}) enters only the bias term, so the risk term has no model-readable component. Risk also takes a growing share of the squared error with scale, to from B to B. At a fabrication H(p_{M}) relaxes within one token while risk persists up to longer, leaving a confident-but-precarious regime that bridges consecutive fabrications ( at B). Contracting that risk at fixed KL\mathrm{KL} removes – of web-verified hallucinations across six rungs and three families. Semantic entropy fires less on that branch (p\\!<\\!10^{-16}) though it carries nearly the fabrications. Bigger models snowball mistakes faster, through a failure mode that is dominant, self-perpetuating, causal and invisible to the model itself.

##  Introduction
The canonical account treats hallucination as a training-time knowledge gap , yet three facts escape it: models fabricate around known facts ; each fabrication makes the next likelier within a single decode ; and decorrelating parallel representations cuts hallucination at _fixed_ data and parameters (Chakrabarti and Balachundhar, 2025). All three implicate novel dynamics that causally mediate hallucination in modern language models.
We hypothesize that hallucination is a guess the model commits to, seeding an auto-regressive risk it can no longer see. To analyze this, we decompose error into _oracle surprise_ (an oracle’s cross-entropy on model predictions), _entropy_ (the model’s uncertainty), and _risk_ (the spread of their disagreement across candidate tokens). Only entropy is self-readable, since a model feels its own uncertainty but neither the oracle’s surprise nor its risk.

Autoregression converts that risk into bias. At fabrication onset the model draws an unsupportable token and conditions on it, freezing momentary risk into fixed bias (Section 2); felt uncertainty relaxes while oracle-referenced risk persists (Section 3.2), and fabrications chain, raising the next claim’s hazard 1.08\times–1.71\times with scale (Section 4.1).
Between consecutive fabrications, the model disproportionately occupies a confident yet precarious regime (low uncertainty, high risk), up to (Section 4.2). Contracting the risk _after_ onset drops rest-of-response fabrication up to 74% across three model families. This reframes detectors that read functionals of (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024; Manakul et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024), which are sharp at onset but blind to the subsequent risk driving downstream fabrications.
We demonstrate our hypothesis in four ways:
  * •
Hallucination Mechanism. The moments of δ=\delta=\log p_{M}-\log p_{O} explain fabrications, and autoregression converts post-onset risk into hallucinatory bias (Section 2).
  * •
Reliability Anti-Scaling. Scaling closes the knowledge gap up to yet grows knowledge degradation up to across three benchmarks, including in-the-wild chat logs; capability and reliability are distinct scaling axes (Section 3).
  * •
Mechanistic Causality. An over-confident risk regime bridges adjacent fabrications; contracting the risk at fixed KL prevents up to of web-verified hallucinations (Section 4).
  * •
Detector Blindness. That same regime structurally evades self-monitoring, leaving -only detectors blind to the fabrications it drives (Section 4.4).

Is hallucination just a knowledge gap? One disagreement variable says no: its moments recover cross-entropy as gap and entropy as uncertainty, plus one they miss — risk. Read mechanistically through autoregression and training protocols, these terms yield four predictions on how fabrications arise, persist, propagate, and evade self-monitoring at scale (Section 2.2–).
###  2.1 One variable’s moments separate gap, uncertainty, and risk
At position , with model p_{M}(\cdot\mid y_{<t}), oracle p_{O}(\cdot\mid y_{<t}), and a candidate token , define the _disagreement variable_ as the log-likelihood ratio  
| =−.\displaystyle\delta_{t}(x)=\log p_{M}(x\mid y_{<t})-\log p_{O}(x\mid y_{<t}).  |  
| --- |  
We write for \delta_{t}(V) at a random draw V\sim p_{M} and \delta(y_{t}) for its realization on the sampled token; moments \mathbb{E}[\cdot] and \mathrm{Var}[\cdot] are taken over V\sim p_{M} unless noted, and every quantity we report is such a moment of .

with the second line following by the standard identity =−\mathrm{KL}(p_{M}\,\|\,p_{O})=H(p_{M},p_{O})-H(p_{M}).
This split is an interpretability statement before it is a statistical one. The _risk_ term \mathrm{Var}[\delta], which depends on at every candidate the model entertains, has no model-readable component. The model’s _felt uncertainty_ H(p_{M}), the one self-readable functional that proxies risk, enters the error only inside the bias term, as the subjective discount on _oracle surprise_.
###  2.2 Risk increasingly dominates error
Although Equation 2 is an algebraic identity, its two terms face different training pressures. Thus, which dominates is an empirical question.
Maximum-likelihood pretraining pressures the forward objective H(p_{\text{data}},p_{M}), and both models approaching p_{\text{data}} closes our mean \mathrm{KL}\to 0. Nothing comparably narrows spread.
{prediction}
[Risk Concentration] As models scale, risk takes a growing share of error \frac{\mathrm{Var}[\delta]}{\mathbb{E}[\delta^{2}]} and concentrates into heavier tails.
###  2.3 Autoregression converts risk into bias
At onset, sampling collapses the lottery p_{M}(\cdot\mid y_{<t}) to one token and appends it, converting that token’s risk (disagreement spread over candidates) to bias (fixed gap) inherited by every later step.
{prediction}
[Autoregressive Conversion] At onset t=t^{\star}, the sampling draw y_{t}^{\star}\sim p_{M} converts risk \mathrm{Var}[\delta] into bias \mathrm{KL}(p_{M}\,\|\,p_{O}).
The two terms then decay differently. H(p_{M}) depends only on local next-token predictability, which fluent continuation restores within a few tokens (Section 3.2). In contrast, \mathrm{Var}[\delta] depends on the model–oracle gap over a premise now fixed in the shared context, which every subsequent token inherits .
{prediction}
[Persistence Asymmetry] For t>t^{\star}, felt uncertainty H(p_{M}) relaxes quickly to baseline while risk \mathrm{Var}[\delta] persists.
###  2.4 Fabrications hide from self-monitoring
The asymmetry has a corollary that reaches past the model. Detectors built on sampling disagreement, predictive and semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024; Manakul et al., 2023) are functionals of , so by Equation 2 they are blind to the risk term. Their lone foothold H(p_{M}) spikes at onset, then relaxes while \mathrm{Var}[\delta] stays high.
{prediction}
[Detector Blindness] A -only detector cannot read risk \mathrm{Var}[\delta] or effectively detect risk-mediated fabrications.

##  Scaling Closes the Knowledge Gap and Grows the Risk
As models scale, where does the error go? We show that as oracle surprise reduces, it re-organizes into risk. Reliability degrades faster than capability improves (Section 3.1), the model’s proxy stops tracking risk after onset (Section 3.2), and risk grows with scale in both share and tail (Section 3.3).
###  3.1 Reliability degrades faster than knowledge improves
Reliability degrades several-fold faster than knowledge improves. Across families, tasks, and verifiers, the initial knowledge gap closes up to 7.0\times while auto-regressive knowledge degradation grows up to (), estimated by a per-claim mixed-effects logistic fit (Table B.2; stat. sig. rungs). The trend extends beyond curated benchmarks: on entities drawn from real user–chatbot conversations (), degradation remains significant at of rungs across all three families (Appendix B). The error reflects not what the model fails to _know_ but how it _commits_ among candidates it already ranks correctly — the risk term of Equation 2.
###  3.2 Decoding breaks the model risk proxy
Felt uncertainty H(p_{M}) is the only self-readable functional of that can proxy decoding risk, and at the onset it tracks it: \mathbb{E}[\delta], H(p_{M}), and \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[\delta]} rise contemporaneously ().
After onset, however, the channels diverge (Section 2.3). The self-readable H(p_{M}) collapses fastest (half-life t_{1/2}<1 tokens across Qwen3), while the oracle-referenced \mathbb{E}[\delta] and \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[\delta]} both require – tokens to decay by 50%. Once appended, the sampled token fixes bias and risk while local fluency quickly recovers; no functional of tracks persistent risk after onset.
These dynamics are robust across oracle scale (14B vs. 32B Qwen3), oracle family (Qwen3 vs. DeepSeek-V3), and oracle inference settings (top-k ∈{1024,2048,4096}\in\\{1024,2048,4096\\}, Appendix D).
###  3.3 Risk worsens with scale
Risk worsens with scale along two axes: its tail and its share of the error. Realized on the model’s outputs, the per-position risk \mathrm{Var}[\delta]_{t} shrinks more slowly than the bias2 term of Equation 2, so its share of the squared error climbs to between B and B (). Simultaneously, the gap grows heavier-tailed, with excess kurtosis of rising 8.9→33.08.9\to 33.0 (). Counterintuitively, a larger model agrees with the oracle more often on average, yet diverges further when it does.

##  A Risk Regime Bridges Fabrications
What does the lingering risk do? Observationally, a fabrication raises the next claim’s hazard (Section 4.1) through a confident yet precarious risk regime on the inter-claim bridge (Section 4.2); causally, contracting that regime cuts downstream fabrications (Section 4.3), which the model’s own signals never register (Section 4.4).

##  Related Work
Error accumulation in generation is not new. name _hallucination snowballing_ , where a model commits early, then fluently defends what it could recognize as false in isolation; later work plants hallucinatory context in vision-language models or ties faithfulness decay to attention dynamics . Such decay is read as exposure bias , treated at training time by scheduled sampling and sequence-level objectives (Ranzato et al., 2016). We give the first on-policy, causal, whole-response test on a frozen model, isolating self-conditioning, a property of decoding not training, as the cause.
Whether that failure is a sampling phenomenon or a knowledge deficit organizes much of the literature (Ji et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2025). Sampling-side evidence is direct, spanning architecture, activations, and decoding. Decorrelating parallel representations cuts hallucination at _fixed_ parameter and data budgets (Chakrabarti and Balachundhar, 2025), injected hidden-activation noise exposes the same variance , and temperature and nucleus truncation trade factuality for diversity (Lee et al., 2022; Holtzman et al., 2020), tunable by hallucination-aware thresholds . Abbasi Yadkori et al. (2024) separate epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty by iterative prompting within one model. We take the trade-off as given and ask what converts onset sampling variance into persistent error, measuring it with an _inter_ -model split against a stronger oracle rather than within the model itself.
Wrong outputs are frequently not missing knowledge. Gekhman et al. (2024) define a _known fact_ as greedy-correct (our definition) and show fine-tuning on unknown facts breeds hallucination; find internal states encode the right answer the model contradicts; show hallucinations strike known facts with high certainty, not sampling noise. Such self-knowledge is also steerable and localizable. Entity latents gate refusal versus hallucination (Ferrando et al., 2025), calibration improves with scale (Kadavath et al., 2022), and non-factual recall localizes to layers . Our decomposition subsumes these as a small bias with non-zero risk, the distributional, decoding-time form of their static, probe-level gap.
Methods already exploit that residual, since sample disagreement is itself a hallucination signal. Predictive uncertainty was an early cue (Xiao and Wang, 2021); semantic entropy clusters generations by meaning to flag confabulations (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024), SelfCheckGPT detects from black-box disagreement (Manakul et al., 2023), linear probes recover it from one generation , and hidden-state covariance scores hallucination directly , as a survey catalogs . These build detectors from our across-sample variance; we add the time-resolved contrast they lack, showing that the self-readable side goes quiet () while risk persists, feeding downstream bias along the committed branch.
Orthogonally, theory-side accounts locate the cause in training rather than decoding. On these accounts, calibrated models must hallucinate on rare facts (Kalai and Vempala, 2024), evaluations reward guessing over abstention , dominant associations overshadow relevant knowledge , and hallucinations emerge with factual knowledge during training (Zucchet et al., 2025). These explain why hallucination is inevitable at pretraining and under current incentives; none predicts a causal self-conditioning effect that _strengthens_ with scale.
The nascent science of reliability casts it as an axis distinct from capability (Rabanser et al., 2026), with larger models growing more capable yet answering confidently wrong and autonomy bounded by reliably-completed task length . These measure it behaviorally and extrinsically, via prompt-perturbation sensitivity and accuracy across a human-difficulty axis. We demonstrate a stronger, mechanistic result inside the model: at a fixed prompt, unreliability intensifies within a response even as scale grows capability, and we causally isolate its origin to self-conditioning, intrinsic to autoregressive decoding.
Our reliability result instantiates _inverse scaling_ , tasks on which larger models do worse (McKenzie et al., 2023). The closest precedents are factual, where larger models score lower on TruthfulQA by imitating human misconceptions and sycophancy strengthens with scale and RLHF . Our degradation adds another, fundamental form that we trace back to a structural aspect of auto-regressive language models: decoding-time self-conditioning.
##  Discussion and Implications
The field treats hallucination today as a knowledge gap. Missing mass, inevitable for rare facts and rewarded by current evaluations, can be bought down by scale (Kalai and Vempala, 2024; Kalai et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025). Detectors catch the residue by reading the model’s uncertainty (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024; Manakul et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024). Both assume error tracks what the model does not know.
Building on Rabanser et al. (2026), we show that not only is reliability an independent axis, but it scales _inversely_ with parameters at a fixed knowledge gap via decoding dynamics. Splitting model–oracle disagreement into gap, uncertainty, and risk (Section 2) separates what a model does not know from how it _decodes_ what it knows. Scale buys knowledge while risk-driven degradation grows (Section 3); contracting risk at fixed KL cuts downstream fabrication across three families (Section 4).
Two open problems follow, split by access and purpose. The detection challenge is black-box: because many detectors read functionals of and Equation 2 shows \mathrm{Var}[\delta] is not one, they underperform on unreliability (Section 4.4). To effectively detect unreliability, increasingly mediated by risk in modern language models, black-box detectors must cheaply carry an external referent.
The interpretability question is white-box: where does risk live and which components mediate it? Early evidence offers clues: our gap-only fit reads risk without labels (Section 3.3) and representation interventions move hallucination at fixed budgets (Chakrabarti and Balachundhar, 2025; Ferrando et al., 2025), though none point to a component. Localizing risk to layers, heads or features would expand our understanding of model behavior under the second moment, which our work implicates as fundamental to reliability.
The failure mode that dominates the error budget, self-perpetuates across a response, and worsens with scale is exactly the one self-monitoring structurally cannot capture. As such, reliability will not arrive for free with capability.
## Limitations
#### The causal test is a ceiling.
The causal test (Section 4.3) uses a co-resident oracle to both detect onset and define the contraction, so it bounds an online variance controller, not a deployable oracle-free rule. The oracle is what isolates the knob: a mean-shifting one (temperature, self-grounding) cannot perturb \mathrm{Var}[\delta] at fixed . It conditions on trajectories that reach an onset, so generalization beyond them is unestablished, though the contrast stays fair — control and intervention draw from one free-run conditional distribution. The effect is same-signed and significant ( CI excludes zero) at all six modelfamily rungs, though OLMo-3 (one rung) and Llama-3.2 (two low- rungs) sample non-Qwen families thinly.
#### Detector comparison is mechanistic.
We do not claim to out-detect named methods. The prediction is structural: every self-readable signal is a functional of (Equation 2), and the one we resolve in time, H(p_{M}), relaxes within a token of onset while oracle-referenced risk persists (). Semantic entropy and SelfCheckGPT, also -functionals, inherit that relaxation; a time-resolved head-to-head on the inter-claim bridge is left open.
#### Decoding and position models.
The moment results are teacher-forced — computed from and directly, not from sampled tokens — hence invariant to the decoding temperature and top- a deployment would choose. Only the free-run analyses (onset, snowball, intervention) depend on decoding, run at each model’s standard nucleus setting; re-running the snowball at greedy and \tau{=}0.5 keeps the across-claim relative risk above and scale-increasing (Table B.3), though a denser sweep would tighten it. Knowledge degradation () is a per-claim logistic GLMM (response-clustered start-to-end rise in hallucination; Table B.2), not a linear-probability slopeintercept ratio, so it avoids the wide intervals that ratio carries at a small start-of-response gap; the _gap_ column remains an OLS intercept.
#### Domain scope.
The evidence is English long-form parametric generation: FActScore biography prompts ( topics) drive the position fit and teacher-forced decompositions, LongFact++ open-domain prompts ( topics) supply the free-run trajectories for the onset, snowball, bridge, and contraction analyses (Appendix A), and WildHallucinations extends the position fit to in-the-wild entities ( categories) judged against curated web corpora (Appendix A). Reasoning chains, source-grounded generation, summarization, dialogue, code, and other languages lie outside this paper’s scope; the moment analyses (Section 2–) are scoped to settings where has a verifiable referent.
#### Verifier.
Claim labels come from a Claude web-search verifier, so verifier error could propagate. Our guard is independent corroboration: the verifier-free white-box risk marker agrees with the labels at AUROC – across scale (Appendix H), and the effects replicate across three families and several metrics. Early positions are the most verifier-sensitive; a human spot check, double-judge pass, and verifier-temperature sweep are open.
## Ethics Statement
This work analyzes an existing failure mode and adds no generative capability. Its intended use is protective: the risk signal (Section 3.3) supports abstention, calibrated confidence, routing risky spans to verification, and transparent disclosure of model reliability. The dual-use surface is that any such signal could be inverted to push fabrications into the high-commitment regime where detectors go quiet (Section 6); two things bound this: the signal needs white-box logit access, and publishing the mechanism aids detection at least as much as evasion. We make no deployed-system, clinical, or legal claims; all data are public benchmarks scored with a public verifier API, at modest single-GPU compute.

#### Datasets.
Three public, English benchmarks carry the analyses, one per measurement role. FActScore biography prompts ( sampled topics) supply long-form generations whose atomic facts are individually verifiable; these drive the position fit and the teacher-forced decompositions. LongFact++ augmented prompts ( topics) supply the free-run hallucinating trajectories the onset and intervention analyses run on. All three are released for research use (FActScore MIT, LongFact Apache-2.0, WildHallucinations for research), as are the Qwen3 weights (Apache-2.0); our non-commercial academic use is consistent with each license, and we collect and release no new data.
#### Verifier protocol.
Claim labels come from a Claude verifier with web search. For a generated response the verifier (i) extracts atomic factual claims, (ii) marks each claim’s token span, and (iii) labels each _supported_ (S) or _not supported_ (U) by issuing web-search queries and checking the claim against retrieved evidence. The _fabrication onset_ is the first token of the first U-labeled claim. For WildHallucinations the referent is closed rather than web-wide: each claim is judged against the dataset’s curated per-entity corpus (the top- pages its authors scraped) by a tool-free Claude call — supported, contradicted-or-absent, or indecisive — with no web search, using the same claim-extraction leaf so granularity is identical across all three evals; indecisive labels are dropped, as throughout. All support/unsupport counts, snowball pairs, and intervention outcomes derive from these labels; systematic verifier error would propagate, so we rely on cross-measurement consistency (a verifier-independent white-box risk marker agrees with the labels at AUROC –, Appendix H) rather than re-validating the verifier here. Double-judge robustness and a verifier-temperature sweep are open.
#### Reproducibility.
Seeds are fixed at the outermost level and per-worker RNG is derived deterministically; cloud runs use aws s3 sync for artifact persistence and resume to a fresh-run-identical state. Every reported number is piped from one computation in the corresponding analysis pipeline and regenerated from its source artifact, never hand-copied. All confidence intervals are bootstrap intervals resampling the unit of analysis (trajectories for the snowball and intervention results; claim-pairs for the prefix-swap), and the source of variability is that resampling.

##  Appendix F The Variance-Contraction Intervention
This section details the on-policy intervention behind Section 4.3 and reports three results the main text compresses: _where_ bias and variance concentrate (the live divergence event, not the verifier label), how the contraction localizes to the inter-claim bridge, and a dose-response that pins the lever to variance. The intervention runs on the LongFact++ free-run trajectories (Appendix A) for Qwen3 (–B, oracle B) and OLMo-3-B (oracle B), n{\approx}17– source trajectories per model.
#### The mean-preserving contraction.
At each step the co-resident oracle supplies the full next-token gap =−\delta(x)=\log p_{M}(x)-\log p_{O}(x), its mean μ==\mu=\mathbb{E}_{p_{M}}[\delta]=\mathrm{KL}(p_{M}\|p_{O}) (the _bias_), and its spread \mathrm{Var}_{p_{M}}[\delta] (the _decoding risk_); the intervention replaces with the contraction q_{\lambda} of Equation 4 (main text). The penalty acts on logits via , so the knob is invariant to a uniform shift of \log p_{M} and reduces to ordinary temperature sharpening only in the degenerate case \delta\equiv\text{const}; otherwise it is strictly a second-moment operation. The dose ( chosen per step so =\mathrm{Var}_{q_{\lambda}}[\delta]=\rho\,\mathrm{Var}_{p_{M}}[\delta]) is comparable across models where a raw is not. Holding the mean discards the _lucky-good_ excursions ( on the supported side of ) symmetrically with the bad ones; removing only the bad ones would improve bias and amount to leaking the oracle’s answer, which Equation 4 deliberately does not do.
#### Solving and .
Both multipliers are found by one-dimensional root-finding over the model nucleus support, exploiting that each target moment is monotone in its knob. Inner solve: for a fixed penalty strength , \mathbb{E}_{q_{\lambda}}[\delta] is strictly increasing in , so the unique with \mathbb{E}_{q_{\lambda}}[\delta]=\mu is bracketed by doubling outward from a spread-scaled guess and refined by Brent’s method (tolerance 10^{-10}, \leq 200 iterations); if the constraint already holds within nats the tilt is skipped (). Outer solve: \mathrm{Var}_{q_{\lambda}}[\delta] is monotone decreasing in (with re-solved inside at each ), so the meeting the dose is bracketed and Brent-refined the same way. At a degenerate position — a near-collapsed support where a moment is unreachable — the solver caps at the bracket endpoint and records the realized var​_​ratio=/​\mathrm{var\\_ratio}=\mathrm{Var}_{q_{\lambda}}[\delta]/\mathrm{Var}_{p_{M}}[\delta] and mean drift honestly rather than aborting the co-resident generate barrier; the manipulation check () reports these realized moments. Cost is a handful of Brent iterations of an O​(|nucleus|)O(|\text{nucleus}|) kernel per fired position, and the controller fires only at the trigger crossings (_not_ every token), so the per-token overhead is negligible against the modeloracle forward pass it rides on.

#### Bias and variance concentrate at the trigger, not the verifier onset.
Per-position bias2 and decoding risk are stored for every draw and plotted against two anchors: the verifier fabrication onset t_{0}^{\star} (first unsupported-claim token) and the live oracle trigger t_{\mathrm{trig}}. ##  Appendix G Detector Implementation
#### Semantic entropy.
The detector we test is a functional of alone — the object Section 2.4 concerns. The blindness test (Section 4.4, ) scores each claim with the original semantic-entropy detector (Kuhn et al., 2023; Farquhar et al., 2024): the off-shelf deberta-large-mnli natural-language-inference model, bidirectional-entailment clustering of resamples, and the discrete cluster-proportion entropy (nats). The resamples are the same-model stochastic draws of each prompt already produced for the free-run trajectories (Appendix A). One adaptation makes it claim-level: for the claim under test, each resample’s NLI stance toward the claim (premise resample, hypothesis claim) ∈{entailment, neutral, contradiction}\in\\{\text{entailment, neutral, contradiction}\\} is its semantic cluster, and the score is the discrete entropy over those clusters; the cluster count is therefore not a free hyperparameter (emergent from entailment, at the claim level). Higher entropy more resample disagreement about the claim a stronger hallucination flag. SelfCheck-Prompt (Manakul et al., 2023), also a -functional over the same draws, is implemented from its canonical leave-one-out consistency prompt.
