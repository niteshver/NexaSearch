#   ⟨65⟩ ACL Anthology Atomic Contribution Claims (ACC) 
**346,010 atomic contribution claims extracted from the abstracts of 80,144 ACL Anthology papers — 423 venues, 1963–2026.**
An _atomic contribution claim_ (ACC) is a single self-contained sentence stating one concrete contribution of a paper: **atomic** (exactly one contribution-bearing proposition), **decontextualized** (pronouns resolved, meta-language removed), and **falsifiable** (a verifiable assertion). Unlike raw abstracts or keywords, ACCs separate what a paper _contributes_ from what it merely _mentions_ — the word "parsing" in the motivation of an LLM paper never becomes a parsing claim.
> _Analogous Process Structure Induction (APSI) framework leverages analogies among processes and conceptualization of sub-event instances to predict whole sub-event sequences of previously unseen open-domain processes._ — from 2020.emnlp-main.119⟨66⟩
This is the full-anthology corpus of the **Drift Inspector** project:
  * 🔭 *_Live demo (extended, 6 _ACL venues):__ <https://hamyrappy.github.io/drift-inspector-acl/>
  * 📄 **EMNLP paper case study instance:** <https://hamyrappy.github.io/drift-inspector-emnlp/>
  * 🛠 **Code (extraction + clustering + inspector):** <https://github.com/Hamyrappy/drift-inspector>
  * 🤗 **Companion dataset (EMNLP 2020–2025, with drift clusters):** emnlp-2020-2025-atomic-claims⟨67⟩

##   ⟨60⟩ How this differs from existing ACL Anthology corpora 
Corpora such as ACL-OCL or the ACL Anthology Corpus redistribute paper text (abstracts/full text) and metadata. This dataset instead provides a **derived semantic unit** : each abstract is decomposed into individually usable contribution statements, suitable for claim-level clustering, retrieval, drift/trend analysis, and science-of-science studies where "what did this paper actually contribute?" is the unit of interest.
##   ⟨61⟩ Dataset creation 
  1. **Corpus.** All ACL Anthology papers with abstracts, enumerated via the official `acl-anthology` library (metadata snapshot: July 2026).
  2. **Extraction.** `openai/gpt-oss-120b` (reasoning effort _medium_), few-shot prompt enforcing atomicity / decontextualization / falsifiability and excluding background, motivation, related work, and raw metric-only claims. One LLM pass per abstract, temperature 0.2. Zero extraction errors across the corpus; the exact prompt (hash `f9a9b434c72c`) ships with the code⟨69⟩.
  3. **Quality monitoring.** An independent LLM judge (`gemini-2.5-flash-lite`, separately prompted) rates a stratified 300-claim sample **96.7% Good**. The judge protocol was itself calibrated on a human-validated EMNLP subset (three annotators, Fleiss' κ = 0.844; judge–human agreement 94.5%, κ = 0.863) — see the companion dataset⟨67⟩ for the human-validation corpus and protocol.


##   ⟨62⟩ Limitations 
  * **LLM-extracted, not human-curated.** Claims can contain extraction errors (hallucinated specifics, under-/over-splitting, background leakage). Quality is judge-monitored on samples, not human-revalidated per row.
  * **Abstract-level.** Contributions that surface only in the paper body are invisible; abstracts also omit negative results and technical nuance.
  * **Coverage is abstract-availability-biased.** Pre-2000s papers rarely have machine-readable abstracts; per-year counts reflect anthology coverage, not publication volume.
  * Claim counts per paper vary with abstract style and venue norms; treat cross-venue frequency comparisons descriptively.


##   ⟨63⟩ Licensing 
The claim texts are LLM-generated derived statements and are released under **CC BY 4.0** , matching the ACL Anthology's license for materials published in or after 2016 (which cover 92.6% of source papers here). Bibliographic metadata (titles, authors, years, URLs) are factual records. Note that the ACL Anthology licenses _materials prior to 2016_ under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; claims derived from pre-2016 abstracts (6.7% of rows) can be excluded by filtering `year >= 2016` if your use requires it. Source abstracts are not redistributed in this dataset. Please attribute the ACL Anthology and cite the underlying papers where appropriate.
