# Title:J.S. Bell's Concept of Local Causality

View PDF HTML (experimental)
            Abstract:  While analyses and explications of Bell's Theorem have been legion, less careful attention has been paid to the locality concept which forms the heart of Bell's work. Indeed, much of the controversy about how to properly interpret Bell's Theorem and its implications turns out to have roots in confusions about Bell's locality criterion. We thus set the theorem temporarily aside and attempt to carefully explain and clarify J.S. Bell's concept of local causality. Special emphasis is placed on seeing the locality criterion as a precise mathematical specification of special relativistic space-time causal structure, and on distinguishing Bell's concept of locality from several other ideas (including determinism, correlation, and signaling) with which it has often been confused. We also explore the ``freedom'' concept which, for Bell, is an additional premise needed to derive or test Bell's Inequality, and deconstruct an influential but erroneous analysis of Bell's locality concept.
