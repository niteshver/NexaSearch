# Title:Diffusion and ballistic transport in clean one-dimensional conductors

View PDF HTML (experimental)
            Abstract:  Diffusion and ballistic transport are normally thought to be mutually exclusive phenomena. The reason is that the notion of diffusion entails inelastic scattering processes which typically lead to current relaxation. While diffusive transport is the rule in generic many-body systems, ballistic transport can be protected in integrable one-dimensional (1D) systems by nontrivial conservation laws. The large diffusive response seen experimentally in nearly ideal realizations of the S=1/2 1D Heisenberg model is therefore puzzling. Here, we show that diffusion is, in fact, universally present in interacting 1D systems subject to a periodic lattice potential and question the conjecture that integrability necessarily leads to ballistic transport. Furthermore, we demonstrate that even at high temperatures current decay can be governed by anomalously large time scales, posing a serious challenge for numerical studies.
