# Are the High-weight Neurons the Important Ones in Image Classification Neural Networks?
\fnmQitao \surChen  \fnmF. Richard \surYu 
###### Abstract
As neural network models for image classification advance, neurons play critical roles in pruning, backdoor defense, and interpretability. Yet existing work lacks clarity on the weight-importance relationship. We address this with a neuron importance assessment method using three experiments: quantifying overlap between high-weight and accuracy-impacting neurons, analyzing high-weight neuron perturbation effects, and testing post-retraining accuracy after high-weight neuron ablation. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and Mini-ImageNet reveal key patterns. Overlap analysis shows top 10% high-weight neurons overlap with important ones by only about 25% at maximum, dropping further in subsequent intervals. Perturbation tests find top 10% high-weight neurons cause 45-80% accuracy degradation under certain operations compared to 3-7% for random perturbations, but a third of them show minimal impact. Ablation-retraining results show removing top 10% high-weight neurons leaves accuracy 10-20% below baseline with no recovery, while ablating top 0.1% allows near-full recovery. Notably, some low-weight intervals show 10-17% degradation when perturbed, comparable to mid-range high-weight neurons. These results confirm not all high-weight neurons are important: their importance is nonlinear. Low-weight neurons also contribute significantly. This challenges weight-importance equivalence, offering refined neuron role insights. It supports applications like encryption prioritizing critical high-weight neurons and pruning removing non-critical ones, advancing neural network analysis.

##  Introduction
As the fundamental building blocks of neural networks, neurons possess unique learning and memory capabilities that serve as the core driving force behind the remarkable performance of these networks. Specifically, the learning capability allows neurons to adjust their connection weights (i.e., synaptic strengths) based on input data and feedback signals, enabling the network to gradually extract discriminative features from complex datasets. On the other hand, the memory capability enables neurons to retain the learned information over time, ensuring the stability of the network’s performance on previously seen tasks. Consequently, research on neurons has proliferated, with applications in areas such as pruning(, ), compression(, ) and adversarial attacks(, , ).
Traditional approaches often consider neurons with large absolute values to be the most important, particularly in the context of neural network pruning, where neurons with smaller absolute values are typically pruned. Recent weight-based pruning methods focus on comparing the importance of filters or generated feature maps within specific convolutional layers, thereby retaining critical components and removing non-essential ones. For example, the absolute values of weights for each convolutional kernel are used as a criterion, with kernels of smaller absolute values being pruned(); the importance of feature maps is ranked according to weight(); output feature importance is measured by the size of zero regions in the activation layer(); and feature map channel importance is assessed using the scaling factor in batch normalization layers(). However, DeepMind() proposed a neuroscience-inspired methodology that evaluates the significance of neuronal groups in deep neural networks through microscopic analysis of neuron ablation effects. The findings demonstrate that interpretable single neurons exhibit no greater functional importance than their less interpretable counterparts. While these explainable units offer partial intuitive understanding, their contribution to overall network performance proves statistically insignificant. This revelation necessitates establishing new evaluation frameworks that objectively quantify importance through quantitative metrics rather than subjective interpretability assessments.
As bridges connecting microscopic mechanisms with macroscopic behaviors, interpreting neuronal population-level coordination and feature encoding facilitates deeper understanding of neural network operations, particularly how local feature combinations enable emergent abstract semantic comprehension. Investigating correlations between neuronal weights and functional significance establishes cross-scale associations: integrating granular neuron-level analysis with system-wide emergent properties could achieve breakthroughs in understanding interactions spanning from local component interactions to global system behaviors.To thoroughly evaluate the correlation between neuron weights and their significance within the model, we designed and implemented the following three experiments.
Analyzing the overlap between high-weight neurons and key accuracy contributors: we quantified and compared the overlap between neurons with high weights and those experimentally validated as significantly impacting model prediction accuracy.
Evaluating model robustness under perturbation strategies: perturbations were applied to high-weight neurons, and the corresponding decline in model accuracy was observed and recorded. This helped us assess the contribution of these high-weight neurons to model accuracy and stability.
Retraining and performance evaluation after removing neurons: neurons from different weight intervals within the neural network were removed, and the remaining parts were retrained to further investigate the intrinsic relationship between weight and neuron importance.
By adopting a holistic perspective to evaluate the importance of all neurons in the network, rather than limiting the analysis to a subset or specific type of neurons, we achieve a more efficient and comprehensive assessment. We conducted experiments on the CIFAR-10 and Mini-ImageNet benchmark datasets. Contrary to traditional perspectives, our experimental results demonstrate that not all neurons within the top 10% of weights can be categorized as important ones, while neurons in the bottom 10% of weights are also capable of exerting a significant impact on model performance. This novel finding aids in deepening our understanding of the internal workings of neural networks, improving methods for analyzing and debugging neural networks, advancing interpretability research, guiding the design and optimization of neural networks, and enhancing the recognition of neural network robustness.
##  Related Work
Evaluating the importance of neurons has long been a pivotal topic in neural network research(,). In this section, we review studies from the fields of interpretability and pruning, with a particular focus on the methodologies that have been employed to select and assess the importance of neurons.
###  2.1 Identification of Key Neurons for Model Interpretability
The interpretability of neurons has been a widely studied area in recent years (, , ). Neurons exhibit capabilities such as nonlinear learning and information storage. Visualizing neuron activations within the network facilitates an understanding of how the model responds to different inputs and processes information. Bau et al.() systematically identified the semantics of neurons in image classification and generation networks by constructing a pixel-level dataset with semantic concepts and introducing the network dissection framework. The importance of neuron feature representation was quantified by evaluating the consistency between hidden layer neurons and semantic concepts. Anh() and Chris O et al.() expanded beyond the construction of semantic datasets by optimizing inputs through synthetic features to activate specific neurons. After identifying particular features corresponding to individual neurons, Hase et al.() localized specific facts to individual neurons and modified the storage of these facts by editing the neurons responsible for this knowledge. Previous studies have employed techniques such as weight masking, feature activation analysis, and ablation experiments() to identify neurons that are critical for various tasks. However, these investigations have not explored the relationship between neurons and their corresponding weights. Conducting in-depth research on whether neurons with larger absolute values constitute a key neuron group is essential for identifying those that play a dominant role in the network’s decision-making process. This not only helps to uncover the intricate information processing mechanisms within neural networks but also enhances the transparency and reliability of model predictions. By precisely identifying these key neurons, we can further explore ways to design more intuitive and interpretable neural network architectures.
###  2.2 Selection of Critical Neurons in Model Pruning
Neural network pruning can be categorized based on granularity into unstructured pruning (, , ) and structured pruning (, ). Unstructured pruning is particularly effective for reducing the memory footprint of neural networks during both training and inference. Notably, neuron-based pruning facilitates a more flexible and tailored final architecture. Among neuron-based methods, Han et al.() proposed an unstructured pruning technique that identifies neurons with absolute weight values below an empirical threshold as unimportant. These neurons are pruned to achieve a sparse network weight pattern. Similarly, Sun et al. () pruned non-essential neurons by evaluating weights with the smallest magnitudes, multiplied by the corresponding input activations for each output. In contrast, structured pruning adopts a hardware-friendly approach by removing convolutional kernels based on importance rankings or similarity. For instance, S.H. Shabbeer Basha et al.() assess filter(combination of neurons) importance during iterative pruning by calculating the similarity between filters. Tianzong Yu et al.() combined the inverse input autocorrelation matrix with the weight matrix to evaluate and prune unimportant input channels or nodes in each CNN layer. This method enables the removal of redundant filters, significantly increasing speed while maintaining a low error rate. Yongbin Z et al.() calculate the importance scores of filters based on their weights, subsequently removing those with low scores considering both their direct and indirect impacts, which leads to improved performance. By eliminating redundant or less contributive neurons, computational costs and storage requirements can be reduced while preserving model performance, thus enhancing overall model efficiency. The prevailing practice has been to prune neurons with smaller weights(,), but it remains unclear whether neurons with larger weights truly exert a significant influence. Previous studies have not provided relevant research or conclusions on this matter.

##  Method
It is generally agreed that, larger weights typically indicate that the corresponding features are more important or appear more frequently in the input data, whereas smaller weights may correspond to less significant features or even noise. However, despite this common assumption, few studies have thoroughly investigated whether neurons with large weights are truly synonymous with important neurons. Prior research on neuron importance, including the work of Bolei Zhou et al. in 2018() and Shlomi Hod et al. in 2021(), has not fully addressed this question. To address this challenge and assess the significance of neurons with large weights, we designed three comprehensive evaluation experiments.

Subsequently, apply the absolute value operation to each weight in the one-dimensional vector to eliminate sign information and retain only the magnitude as shown in Figure 1A(b). Finally, sort the absolute values to generate an initial neuron weight matrix as shown in Figure 1A(c). Based on this preprocessing, we designed three experiments as shown in Figure 1B:
As shown in Figure 1B (a1) and (a2), we quantified and compared the overlap between high-weight neurons and those experimentally validated as having a significant impact on model prediction accuracy. This aimed to analyze whether there is a consistency between high-weight neurons and key accuracy contributors.
As illustrated in Figure 1B (b1) and (b2), to assess the contribution of neurons with different weights to model accuracy and stability, we conducted a neuron perturbation experiment: perturbations were introduced to neurons, and the resulting decline in model accuracy was closely monitored and recorded, thus achieving the aforementioned assessment goal.
As depicted in Figure 1B (c1) and (c2), we performed a neuron removal and retraining experiment. Neurons from different weight intervals were removed from the network, and the remaining neurons were retrained, aiming to further uncover the relationship between weight magnitude and neuron importance.
###  3.2 Analyzing the Overlap Between High-Weight Neurons and Key Accuracy Contributors
In the context of a trained neural network, we adopt a systematic approach to identify critical neurons by iteratively ablating each neuron within the network’s architecture. Specifically, for each layer of the weight matrix, we conduct a granular analysis by sequentially setting the weights of individual neurons to zero. This method effectively simulates the removal of a single neuron while maintaining the integrity of the network’s structure and the parameters of other neurons.
Following each ablation, we evaluate the modified network using an independent test dataset. By measuring key performance metrics such as accuracy, we quantify the impact of each neuron’s removal on the model’s predictive capabilities. Through comprehensive comparisons of the model’s performance before and after each ablation, we are able to pinpoint neurons that exert a substantial influence on the overall output. These neurons are deemed critical, as their absence leads to significant degradation in the model’s accuracy or other performance indicators.
After training the neural network, we take the absolute values of the weights and sort them in descending order, selecting the top n neurons as the weight importance TOP-n set . We performe ablation on individual neurons and evaluate the network’s accuracy after each neuron is ablated. The neurons with the highest impact on accuracy are defined as the accuracy impact TOP-n set . Finally, the overlap ratio between is calculated these two sets, which is measuring the proportion of neurons in the Accuracy Impact TOP-n set that also appear in the Weight Importance TOP-n set .

###  3.4 Retraining and Performance Evaluation After Removing High-Weight Neurons
In contrast to previous approaches, both neuron pruning and neuron freezing strategies are simultaneously applied to assess the importance of neurons across different weight intervals(Algorithm 1). Specifically, for the trained neural network, the absolute values of the neuron weights are sorted in descending order, and the weights of the Top-n neurons are set to zero. A mask matrix is then employed to ensure that the gradients and weights of these neurons remain unchanged during subsequent model retraining, allowing only the unfrozen parameters to be updated. This approach aims to investigate the contribution of different neuron weight regions to the model’s learning capability and their potential impact on the final model accuracy.
To further validate the effectiveness of the partitioning strategy and its specific impact on model performance, a comparative experiment is designed. In this experiment, the neurons in the neural network are randomly selected, and the same strategies are applied to these neurons. This random freezing strategy is intended to simulate an unstructured parameter reduction method, evaluating its general impact on the model training process and final accuracy. ##  Experiment
###  4.1 Experiment Setup
Two image classification datasets, CIFAR-10() and Mini-ImageNet() were used to train our models. CIFAR-10 comprising 50000 training images and 10000 validation images, features a 10-class image classification task with images sized at 32\times 32 Mini-ImageNet boasts 60,000 color images, each sized at 84\times 84, divided into 100 classes, with 600 examples per class. The image was reshaped to 224\times 224 pixels and normalized with a mean of 0.5 and a variance of 0.5 before being used as input for the models. The number of images in each dataset was divided into training(80%), validation(10%) and test(10%) sets. The experiments were conducted in parallel using four NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPUs. Adam optimizer was utilized to train all models with Initial learning rate of 0.0001. The number of training and retraining iterations were respectively set to 50 and 20. The selected LeNet-5() is the basis of the earliest CNN models which applied in the field of computer vision. Its success and application inspired the development of more complex and deeper convolutional neural network architectures that followed. Furthermore, it has a relatively small number of parameters, allowing for a noticeable impact on accuracy when adjusting individual neurons.

###  4.3 The Model Robustness Under Perturbation Strategies
After partitioning neuron weights into ten intervals with 10% increments each, we randomly sampled 10% of neurons and conducted ten independent perturbation experiments, using the average result as a benchmark. We then designed a series of subfigures to systematically explore perturbation effects across different models and datasets, with each subsequent experiment building on the insights from the previous one.
Starting with Figure 3A, which focuses on the ResNet-18 model on the CIFAR-10 dataset, we aimed to establish a baseline for perturbation effects under both constant (Plus and Minus) and proportional (Multiply and Divide) perturbation operations. The results showed distinct patterns: high-weight neurons in the top 10% interval exhibited pronounced vulnerability under equal-value perturbation, accuracy dropped sharply even with only 5% weight disturbance, while under proportional perturbation, accuracy declined rapidly as intensity increased. Other intervals showed negligible accuracy changes, laying the foundation for further validation across different datasets.
To verify whether these patterns hold across datasets, we conducted the experiment in Figure 3B, focusing on ResNet-18 on Mini-ImageNet. Consistent with Figure 3A, top 10% high-weight neurons remained highly vulnerable to perturbations. Notably, a new trend emerged: in equalized perturbation (e.g., fixed 0.04 perturbation), the lowest 10% weight neurons experienced a steep accuracy drop to 10%, indicating performance degradation when low-weight perturbations fall below a threshold. This dataset-specific observation prompted us to check if model architecture influences such trends.
To explore the impact of model architecture, we designed Figure 3C using the ResNet-50 model on CIFAR-10. The results confirmed the generalizability of key trends from ResNet-18: top 10% high-weight neurons remained vulnerable, and low-weight intervals showed threshold-dependent degradation. However, subtle differences in perturbation sensitivity (e.g., magnitude of accuracy drops) highlighted architecture-specific nuances, motivating a final validation across both architecture and dataset.
Figure 3D, focusing on ResNet-50 on Mini-ImageNet, aimed to consolidate findings across both models and datasets. It reinforced the core patterns: high vulnerability of top 10% high-weight neurons, threshold effects in low-weight intervals, and consistent negligible changes in mid-range intervals. Together, these four subfigures demonstrated the robustness of perturbation effect patterns across models and datasets.

Low-weight intervals exhibit threshold-dependent degradation, with accuracy plummeting when perturbations fall below a certain level. Together with Tables 1 and 2, these results confirm that neuron weight correlates with importance in high-weight ranges but shows weak or inconsistent links in lower ranges, highlighting the complexity of the relationship between weight and functional importance.

###  4.4 Performance Evaluation After Removing High-Weight Neurons and Retraining
On CIFAR-10 and Mini-ImageNet, we divided weights of LeNet, ResNet-18, and ResNet-50 into 10 equal intervals, applied neuron ablation to each, and fine-tuned the models. Ten control experiments with random neuron ablation ensured result validity.
Starting with Figure 4A, we focused on ResNet-18 on the CIFAR-10 dataset to establish a baseline for ablation effects across weight intervals. The results showed a clear pattern: removing the top-10% absolute-weight neurons left the model?s performance stuck at the random ablation baseline even after 20 epochs of retraining, while ablation of other weight intervals allowed the model to reallocate weights via retraining, recovering test accuracy and sometimes exceeding the initial baseline. This highlighted the unique irreplaceability of top-10% neurons in ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10.
To verify whether this pattern holds across datasets, we conducted the experiment in Figure 4B, focusing on ResNet-18 on Mini-ImageNet. This step aimed to rule out dataset-specific biases and confirm if the top-10% neurons is consistent across data distributions. The results mirrored Figure 4A: top-10% ablation trapped performance at the random baseline, while other intervals recovered, reinforcing that the trend is not limited to CIFAR-10.
Next, to explore the impact of model architecture, we designed Figure 4C using ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10. Having observed consistent results in ResNet-18 across datasets, we sought to check if larger, more complex architectures exhibit the same behavior. Figure 4C confirmed that ResNet-50 also showed stagnant performance after top-10% ablation, with other intervals recovering, indicating the pattern is not unique to ResNet-18 but generalizes to deeper architectures.
To finalize the validation across both architecture and dataset, Figure 4D focused on ResNet-50 on Mini-ImageNet. This experiment aimed to confirm that the trend persists when both model and dataset are varied, ensuring the findings are robust. The results aligned with Figures 4A-C: top-10% ablation prevented recovery, while other intervals regained performance, solidifying the generality of the top-10% neurons? irreplaceability.
Subdividing the top 10% into five smaller intervals (0.01%-0.1% by ​e​d​u​c​t​i​o​n10\times reduction), Figure 4A-D further revealed nuanced patterns: Except for the 0.01%-0.1% subinterval in ResNet-18 and ResNet-50, ablation of neurons in other top-10% subintervals unlike the broader top-10% allowed full performance recovery via fine-tuning. Across all four figures, LeNet (when included) showed a distinct but related trend: its performance dropped significantly after top-10% ablation but did not reach random-guessing levels, indicating architectural differences in weight sensitivity. Collectively, Figure 4A-D demonstrate that the top-10% absolute-weight neurons (specifically excluding the 0.01%-0.1% subinterval in deeper ResNets) are uniquely irreplaceable across models and datasets, while other weight intervals can be compensated for via retraining.
###  4.5 Discussion
This study uses neuron ablation, perturbation, and ablation-retraining experiments to quantify the non-trivial relationship between neuron weights and their importance, revealing nuanced roles of high-weight neurons and critical contributions from low-weight neuron collectives.
Overlap Analysis shows top-weight neurons dominate individual importance, but lower-weight intervals exhibit increasing collective contribution. Subdividing high-overlap intervals reveals an importance gradient even among relatively high-weight neurons, suggesting low-weight neurons encode widespread feature combinations vital for model functionality, while high-weight neurons vary in task-relevant feature encoding.
Perturbation Testing demonstrates top 10% weight neurons are highly sensitive-small perturbations drastically degrade performance by disrupting key recognition features. Notably, some low-weight neurons also critically impact performance when perturbed, likely due to their roles in bridging essential feature combinations, despite minimal weight magnitudes and training variations. Ablation-Retraining Experiments find removing the broad top 10% high-weight neurons causes irreversible performance loss, but ablating only the top 0.1% (after subdivision) allows full recovery via fine-tuning. This indicates these extreme high-weight neurons occupy local critical nodes in information pathways, yet the model can reconstruct effective processing through other neurons’ weight adjustments.
Overall, this work rejects the monotonic weight-importance assumption: while high-weight neurons often hold individual significance, low-weight neurons’ collective interactions and strategic connectivity make them indispensable. In addition to neuronal weights, the contribution of neurons to the output results also be influenced by factors such as their position within the network, the manner of inter-neuronal connections, the initial weight values, and the training process.
Nevertheless, this research also has limitations, such as being specific to certain model application domains and having a single analytical dimension. In the future, we aim to extend this research to diverse domains such as object detection and natural language processing, to further validate and deepen our understanding of the complex relationship between neuron weight and importance. Additionally, we plan to adopt a more granular level of analysis, focusing on modules or convolutional layers, to eliminate potential biases in weight distribution across different structural units.
##  Conclusion
This study primarily presents three strategies to quantify the relationship between neuron weights and their importance: weighted coverage analysis to examine the overlap between high-weight neurons and highly important neurons; perturbation experiments to measure model performance changes after neuron perturbations; and ablation experiments involving removing high-weight neurons, retraining the model, and assessing the impact on remaining performance. While high-weight neurons play a key role in enhancing model robustness and maintaining high accuracy compared to other weight intervals, their importance is not monotonically consistent, some high-weight neurons are not critical to overall performance. Meanwhile, certain low-weight neurons also significantly impact model performance. This work holds substantial value: it provides a new perspective and quantitative method for understanding the relationship between neuron weights and importance, enriching the theoretical foundation of artificial intelligence. It supports the establishment of importance-aware hierarchical encryption mechanisms, aiding in defending against pre-training weight contamination in model encryption. Additionally, it facilitates more precise exploration of the intrinsic connection between neuron weights and their importance in specific tasks, with implications for addressing issues like backdoor implantation and parameter tampering under controllable performance loss.

###  A.1 Generalization Experiment Setup
In addition to open-source data, to further validate our conclusions, we collected real financial data to construct our own dataset and conducted experiments on this dataset. The specific implementations are as follows:
Through the JoinQuant quantitative investment and research platform, we collected 50 futures contracts from major domestic trading markets such as the China Financial Futures Exchange, Shanghai Futures Exchange, and Dalian Commodity Exchange, fully covering historical daily data from 2003 to 2024.
Given the inherent time constraints of futures contracts?that is, the limited contract holding period and the mandatory delivery at maturity?there are significant overlapping characteristics among different futures contracts in the temporal dimension. To ensure the continuity and completeness of time-series analysis, the preprocessing of futures contracts is particularly important. The strategy adopted in this study is as follows: First, the acquired futures contract data are spliced in chronological order, and the contract with the largest daily trading volume is selected as the main contract for that day, serving as the primary representative of market trends. Subsequently, to eliminate abnormal price fluctuations caused by irregular contract changes, this study introduces median filtering technology as a preliminary smoothing method, then plots the contract month change diagram through MATLAB, followed by manual review and adjustment, especially at the switching points of main contracts, to ensure data continuity and accuracy.
In terms of data visualization, this study follows the graphic design principles of industry-standard websites such as Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance to construct major price charts(Fig. 1). The image sequence designed in this study is composed of OHLC bar charts at 20-day intervals (monthly strategy). By setting a unified image height and dynamically adjusting the vertical axis ratio, the highest and lowest points of the OHLC path in each image are precisely aligned with the top and bottom edges of the image. This design strategy ensures that the price ranges of all securities are uniformly scaled to the same height, eliminating differences in absolute prices across securities, allowing the model to focus more on relative price changes, and enhancing the consistency of cross-asset predictions.
